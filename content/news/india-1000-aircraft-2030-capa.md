According to the latest research report published by CAPA India (Centre for Aviation), the operational commercial aircraft fleet in India is on track to cross **1,000 active aircraft by early 2030**, up from approximately 700 airframes in 2024.

### The Crew Supply Challenge

The report emphasizes that the primary constraint facing Indian aviation is not capital or passenger demand, but rather the availability of certified, high-caliber aviation personnel:
- **Cockpit Crew Requirements:** Maintaining 1,000+ active commercial aircraft requires a national pool of over 12,000 active airline pilots adhering to DGCA Flight Duty Time Limitations (FDTL).
- **Cabin Crew Requirements:** Airlines will need to onboard over 2,500 new flight attendants annually to support new deliveries and route expansion.
- **Flight School Capacity:** Domestic flight training capacity in India must expand alongside reliable international training partnerships to prevent pilot supply bottlenecks.

To be eligible for a [Commercial Pilot Licence](https://avioraaviation.in/blog/what-is-cpl-commercial-pilot-license) in India, you must be at least eighteen years old, have passed 10+2 with Physics and Mathematics from a recognised board or an equivalent qualification, hold a valid DGCA medical certificate, and be able to communicate in English. Students from Commerce or Arts backgrounds can still qualify by completing Physics and Mathematics through a recognised open schooling route such as NIOS.[ For the complete journey from school to licence, read how to become a pilot after 12th.](https://avioraaviation.in/blog/how-to-become-a-pilot-after-12th)

 

## CPL Eligibility at a Glance

 

Eligibility for a CPL rests on four things: your age, your educational background, your medical fitness, and your English proficiency. Each of these is checked before training begins, and each one matters for the final licence, so it is worth confirming all four early.

 

### Age Requirement

 

You must be at least eighteen years old to be issued a Commercial Pilot Licence. Many students begin ground classes at seventeen or eighteen, soon after finishing school, so that they reach the minimum age as their training progresses.

 

### Educational Requirement

 

The standard requirement is a pass in 10+2 with Physics and Mathematics from a recognised board, or an equivalent qualification. Many training organisations also look for a minimum aggregate score, commonly around fifty percent, so it is worth asking your chosen academy what it requires.

 

If you studied Commerce, Arts, or Humanities and did not take Physics and Mathematics, you are not shut out. You can complete these two subjects through the National Institute of Open Schooling, which is widely accepted for this purpose, and then proceed with CPL training.

 

### Medical Requirement

 

A valid DGCA medical certificate is required, and a Class 1 medical is the one needed for a Commercial Pilot Licence. It is conducted by a DGCA approved medical examiner and checks vision, hearing, heart health, and general fitness. Because this is the requirement most likely to stop a candidate, it is wise to complete it before spending money on training.

 

### English Proficiency

 

All ground examinations and flight communication are in English, so pilots must be able to read, speak, and understand it well. Radio communication standards are formally assessed as part of the licensing process.

 

### Documents Commonly Required

 

The exact list can vary by academy and by the candidate's profile, but students are usually asked to prepare the following.

 

* Class 10 certificate as proof of date of birth.
* Class 12 mark sheet showing Physics and Mathematics, or NIOS mark sheets if those subjects were completed later.
* Valid government photo identification such as Aadhaar and a passport.
* Recent passport size photographs.
* DGCA medical certificate.
* Any additional forms or records requested by the academy or by the DGCA.

 

Requirements and forms are updated from time to time, so always confirm the current list with the DGCA and your training academy before you apply.

 

## Frequently Asked Questions

 

### Can Commerce or Arts students become commercial pilots?

 

Yes. Students from these backgrounds can become commercial pilots by completing Physics and Mathematics through NIOS or another recognised open schooling route before or alongside their CPL ground training.

### &#x20;

### Is there an upper age limit for a CPL?

 

The DGCA does not set a strict upper age limit for CPL training, though airlines may have their own hiring preferences, so starting earlier generally helps.

 

### Should I do the medical examination before joining a flying school?

 

Yes. A medical problem discovered after you have paid for ground classes or flying hours can be costly, so most advisors recommend clearing the medical first.

The DGCA Class 2 medical is the entry level aviation medical assessment that aspiring pilots in India complete before starting flight training. It is required for a Student Pilot Licence and for a Private Pilot Licence, and for most students it is the first medical step before the Class 1 medical that a Commercial Pilot Licence needs. It is a simpler examination than Class 1, but it is still done by a DGCA approved examiner and recorded through the DGCA system. ## What Is the DGCA Class 2 Medical?

&#x20;

The Class 2 medical checks that you are physically and mentally fit to begin learning to fly. It covers your general health, vision, and hearing against the standards set for student and private pilots. Passing it results in a medical assessment that you will need to show your flying school and the DGCA.

&#x20;

## Who Needs a Class 2 Medical?

&#x20;

Anyone planning to hold a Student Pilot Licence needs a valid Class 2 medical assessment, and so does anyone applying for a Private Pilot Licence. Aspiring commercial pilots usually take it first, because it lets them begin flight training and move on to the more demanding Class 1 examination afterwards.

&#x20;

## Class 2 and Class 1: How They Differ

&#x20;

Class 2 is for student and private flying, while Class 1 is required for a Commercial Pilot Licence and for flying professionally. Class 1 is more detailed and, for initial examinations, is carried out only at designated centres, while Class 2 can be done with DGCA approved Class 2 examiners in many cities. Think of Class 2 as the starting point and Class 1 as the professional standard. ## How to Get Your Class 2 Medical

&#x20;

The process generally follows a few simple steps.

&#x20;

### Step 1: Create Your eGCA Account

&#x20;

Register on the DGCA's eGCA portal and keep your login details safe, because you will use them for medicals and licensing later on.

&#x20;

### Step 2: Choose a DGCA Approved Examiner

&#x20;

The DGCA publishes a list of approved Class 2 medical examiners on its website. Pick one near you and book an appointment.

&#x20;

### Step 3: Attend the Examination

&#x20;

The examiner carries out the medical, checking your vision, hearing, and general health, and records the result on the system.

&#x20;

### Step 4: Receive Your Assessment

&#x20;

Once your assessment is approved, you can use it to apply for your Student Pilot Licence and begin training.

&#x20;

## What to Bring

&#x20;

Carry your identity proof, recent passport size photographs, and any spectacle prescription if you wear glasses or lenses. Your examiner will tell you if anything else is needed.

&#x20;

## How Long Is It Valid?

&#x20;

Validity depends on your age, and training organisations commonly quote about two years for candidates under forty and a shorter period for older candidates. Rules are updated from time to time, so confirm the current validity on the DGCA website and renew before it expires.

&#x20;

## How Much Does It Cost?

&#x20;

Fees are set by the examiner or clinic and are generally a few thousand rupees, though they vary by city. Ask when you book your appointment.

&#x20;

## Frequently Asked Questions

&#x20;

### Can I take the Class 2 medical before finishing 12th?

&#x20;

Many students do, since it helps them find out early whether there is any medical issue that could affect a flying career. Check the age requirements for the licence you are aiming for.

&#x20;

### Is Class 2 enough to become a commercial pilot?

&#x20;

No. It is the first step. A Commercial Pilot Licence requires a valid Class 1 medical assessment.

&#x20;

### What if I do not pass the Class 2 medical?

&#x20;

It depends on the finding. Some candidates need further tests or have conditions attached, and others may be assessed as unfit. If you have a specific health concern, speak to a DGCA approved aviation medical examiner for proper advice.

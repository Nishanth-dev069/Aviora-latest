The complete journey from 12th grade to flying commercially for an airline typically takes two to three years and follows six distinct stages. These are confirming eligibility, completing the DGCA Class 1 Medical, finishing ground classes, logging flight training hours, earning a Commercial Pilot Licence, and finally completing a Type Rating. This guide walks through the full roadmap stage by stage.

 

## Stage 1: Confirm Eligibility Before You Commit Financially

 

Before enrolling anywhere, complete 10+2 in any stream, confirm that you meet the DGCA's age requirements, and arrange a preliminary medical check. Catching any potentially disqualifying condition at this stage, rather than after enrolling in a ground school, saves both time and money. This stage applies equally regardless of academic background, including for [commerce or arts students becoming pilots](https://avioraaviation.in/blog/can-commerce-arts-students-become-pilots).

 

## Stage 2: DGCA Class 1 Medical Certificate

 

This stage functions as a hard gate in the process. It is worth getting fully cleared before committing financially to any ground school or flight training program, since your medical fitness ultimately determines whether the rest of the pathway is even viable.

 

## Stage 3: DGCA Ground Classes

 

Ground school covers Air Navigation, Aviation Meteorology, Air Regulations, Technical General, and Technical Specific, and typically takes between six and twelve months to complete. This is where the theoretical foundation for your entire flying career is built and formally examined.

 

## Stage 4: Flight Training and Logging Your Hours

 

The DGCA requires a minimum of two hundred flying hours for CPL eligibility, including specific requirements for cross country flying, instrument time, and night flying. Depending on aircraft availability, training slots, and overall cost, students often complete this stage either within India or through partner flight schools abroad, most commonly in the United States. This stage generally takes between six and twelve months. ## Stage 5: CPL Examinations and Licence Issuance

 

Once ground subjects and flight hours are both complete, candidates sit for the DGCA's final written examinations along with a practical flying skill test. Successfully passing these results in the issuance of a Commercial Pilot Licence, the point at which a candidate is legally permitted to be paid to fly.

 

## Stage 6: Type Rating, the Final Step Before an Airline Job

 

Holding a Commercial Pilot Licence alone does not qualify a pilot to fly a specific commercial aircraft. A Type Rating, such as an A320 or Boeing 737 certification, is required and must match the aircraft operated by a candidate's future employer. Most airlines either require this certification before hiring or sponsor it directly as part of a structured cadet program. ## Stage 7: Airline Interviews and Placement

 

With both a Commercial Pilot Licence and Type Rating secured, the final stage involves airline interviews, simulator assessments, and psychometric testing. This stage is genuinely competitive, but highly achievable for candidates with strong ground knowledge and solid flying fundamentals. ## Total Timeline and Cost Snapshot

 

Most students complete the full pathway from 12th grade to being airline ready within twenty four to thirty six months. Total cost typically ranges from twenty five to forty five lakh rupees for the Commercial Pilot Licence, with an additional fifteen to twenty five lakh rupees required for Type Rating, depending on the aircraft type.

 

## Frequently Asked Questions

 

### What is the difference between CPL and Type Rating?

 

A Commercial Pilot Licence is the foundational qualification that allows a pilot to fly any aircraft category they have trained on. A Type Rating is an additional certification required specifically to fly a particular commercial aircraft model, such as the A320.

 

### Can I skip Type Rating and get hired directly with only a CPL?

 

Most full service and low cost carriers require Type Rating either before or immediately after hiring. Some airlines run cadet programs that include sponsored Type Rating for selected candidates as part of the offer.

 

### How many years does the entire process take from start to finish?

 

Most students complete the full pathway from 12th grade to being airline ready within two to three years, depending on how quickly medical clearance is obtained, how consistently ground school is completed, and the availability of flight training slots.

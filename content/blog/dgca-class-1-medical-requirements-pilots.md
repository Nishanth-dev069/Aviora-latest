The DGCA Class 1 medical is the aviation medical examination that every commercial pilot in India must pass in order to hold a [Commercial Pilot Licence](https://avioraaviation.in/blog/what-is-cpl-commercial-pilot-license). It checks vision, hearing, heart health, and general fitness to make sure a pilot can fly safely. Aspiring pilots usually take a Class 2 medical first and then move on to the initial Class 1 medical, so it helps to understand the whole sequence before you begin. ## What Is the DGCA Class 1 Medical?

&#x20;

The Class 1 medical is the highest grade of civil aviation medical assessment. It is required for pilots who will fly commercially, because airline and charter flying place greater demands on a pilot's health than private flying does. Passing it results in a medical assessment that stays valid for a set period and must be renewed on time to keep your licence active.

&#x20;

## Class 2 First, Then Class 1

&#x20;

For most new students, the sequence begins with a Class 2 medical. The initial Class 1 medical is generally taken by candidates who already hold a valid Class 2 assessment. Because rules and procedures are updated from time to time, check the current DGCA guidance and your academy's advice before booking anything.

&#x20;

## Where Is the Initial Class 1 Medical Done?

&#x20;

Initial Class 1 medicals are conducted at designated centres. These include Indian Air Force medical establishments, such as the Air Force Central Medical Establishment in New Delhi and the Institute of Aerospace Medicine in Bengaluru, as well as a small number of DGCA empanelled civil hospitals. Renewal medicals can usually be done with DGCA empanelled Class 1 examiners, although some cases are handled only at specified centres. The list of centres changes over time, so always confirm on the DGCA website before you book.

&#x20;

## What Does the Class 1 Medical Check?

&#x20;

The examination is thorough and covers several areas.

&#x20;

### Vision and Colour Perception

&#x20;

Your distance and near vision are tested, and your vision must meet the required standard, with or without correction. Colour perception is also tested, since pilots must recognise signals and cockpit indications correctly.

&#x20;

### Hearing and ENT

&#x20;

Hearing and the health of your ear, nose, and throat are checked, because clear communication with air traffic control and crew is essential.

&#x20;

### Heart and General Health

&#x20;

An ECG and a general physical examination assess your cardiovascular health. Blood and urine tests, blood pressure, height, weight, and body mass index are also recorded.

&#x20;

### Neurological and Mental Fitness

&#x20;

The examiner also looks at neurological health and general mental wellbeing, since a pilot must be alert and stable under pressure.

&#x20;

## How Long Is a Class 1 Medical Valid?

&#x20;

Validity depends on the pilot's age and the type of flying, and it is typically one year for younger pilots and shorter for older ones. You should renew within the permitted window before expiry. Renewing too early or too late can require extra approvals, so keep track of your expiry date and plan ahead.

&#x20;

## How to Prepare

&#x20;

Get a good night's sleep and avoid heavy meals or stimulants before the examination. Carry your identity documents, photographs, and the paperwork required for your appointment. If you wear glasses or contact lenses, bring them. It also helps to book your appointment early, since slots at some centres fill quickly.

&#x20;

## Why You Should Not Wait Until the End of Training

&#x20;

A medical problem found late can be expensive, because you may already have paid for ground classes and flying. Many advisors recommend completing the required medicals early, before committing to the full cost of training, so you know where you stand.

&#x20;

## Frequently Asked Questions

&#x20;

### Is the Class 1 medical required for a Commercial Pilot Licence?

&#x20;

Yes. A valid Class 1 medical assessment is required to hold and exercise the privileges of a Commercial Pilot Licence.

&#x20;

### What happens if I do not pass the Class 1 medical?

&#x20;

Depending on the finding, some candidates are deferred for further tests, some receive a certificate with conditions, and others may be assessed as unfit. If you are unsure about a specific health matter, speak to a DGCA approved aviation medical examiner, who can advise you properly.

&#x20;

### Can I take the medical at any hospital?

&#x20;

No. Class 1 medicals must be done at designated centres or with DGCA empanelled examiners, so check the current list before you book.
